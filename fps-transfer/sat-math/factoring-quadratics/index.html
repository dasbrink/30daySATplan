Factoring Quadratic Expressions

SAT Math · Advanced Math · Updated October 2026

Where I would start

Always take out a common factor first. Then, for $x^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$. When the leading coefficient is not $1$, use the $ac$ method: split the middle term and factor by grouping.

Learn two patterns by sight: the difference of squares, $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$, and perfect squares such as $x^2 + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)^2$.

The SAT asks you to choose an equivalent factored form, to find a factor of a quadratic, or to find a missing constant when two forms are equal for all $x$. Any factored answer can be checked by multiplying it back out.

Reading the concept isn't enough. The score comes from practicing in the real digital SAT format and finding the exact mistakes you keep making.

A sign tip: if $c$ is positive, both numbers have the same sign as $b$. If $c$ is negative, the numbers have opposite signs, and the larger one carries the sign of $b$.

How to solve one, step by step

Example 1: a negative constant

Factor $x^2 - 5x - 24$.

  1. You need a product of $-24$ and a sum of $-5$. The product is negative, so the signs are opposite.
  2. The pairs for $24$ are $1 \cdot 24$, $2 \cdot 12$, $3 \cdot 8$, $4 \cdot 6$. The pair $-8$ and $3$ sums to $-5$.
  3. $x^2 - 5x - 24 = (x - 8)(x + 3)$. Check: $x^2 + 3x - 8x - 24 = x^2 - 5x - 24$.

Example 2: factoring by grouping

Factor $6x^2 + x - 2$.

  1. Multiply $a \cdot c = 6 \cdot (-2) = -12$. Find two numbers with product $-12$ and sum $1$: $4$ and $-3$.
  2. Split the middle term: $6x^2 + 4x - 3x - 2$.
  3. Group: $2x(3x + 2) - 1(3x + 2)$.
  4. Factor out $3x + 2$: $(2x - 1)(3x + 2)$.

Example 3: common factor, then a pattern

Factor $2x^2 - 50$ completely.

  1. Take out the common factor $2$: $2(x^2 - 25)$.
  2. $x^2 - 25$ is a difference of squares: $(x - 5)(x + 5)$.
  3. $2x^2 - 50 = 2(x - 5)(x + 5)$.

Example 4: finding a missing constant

If $x^2 + kx - 12 = (x - 2)(x + m)$ for all values of $x$, what is $k$?

  1. Expand the right side: $x^2 + mx - 2x - 2m = x^2 + (m - 2)x - 2m$.
  2. Match the constants: $-2m = -12$, so $m = 6$.
  3. Match the $x$ terms: $k = m - 2 = 4$.

The mistakes that cost points

  • Skipping the common factor. $2x^2 - 50$ is not fully factored until the $2$ comes out.
  • Getting the signs backwards. $(x + 8)(x - 3)$ multiplies to $x^2 + 5x - 24$, not $x^2 - 5x - 24$. Multiply back to check.
  • Factoring a sum of squares. $x^2 + 9$ does not factor over the real numbers. Only a difference of squares does.
  • Confusing factors and zeros. The factor $(x - 3)$ means the zero is $x = 3$, not $x = -3$.
  • Forgetting the leading coefficient. $3x^2 - 11x - 4$ is not $(x - 4)(x + 1)$. The $3$ has to appear in one of the factors.

  • A$(x + 2)(x + 6)$
  • B$(x + 3)(x + 4)$
  • C$(x + 1)(x + 12)$
  • D$(x - 3)(x - 4)$
Show solution
Answer: B. You need two numbers with a product of $12$ and a sum of $7$: $3$ and $4$. Choice A multiplies to $12$ but adds to $8$.

Medium
Which expression is equivalent to $6x^2 + x - 2$?
  • A$(2x - 1)(3x + 2)$
  • B$(2x + 1)(3x - 2)$
  • C$(6x - 1)(x + 2)$
  • D$(3x - 1)(2x + 2)$
Show solution
Answer: A. Use the $ac$ method: $6 \cdot (-2) = -12$, and $4$ and $-3$ multiply to $-12$ and add to $1$. Then $6x^2 + 4x - 3x - 2 = 2x(3x + 2) - 1(3x + 2) = (2x - 1)(3x + 2)$. Choice B expands to $6x^2 - x - 2$.

  • A$2(x - 5)^2$
  • B$2(x - 5)(x + 5)$
  • C$(2x - 25)(x + 2)$
  • D$2(x^2 - 25)$
Show solution
Answer: B. Take out $2$: $2(x^2 - 25)$. Then factor the difference of squares: $2(x - 5)(x + 5)$. Choice D is equivalent but not completely factored.

Hard
Which of the following is a factor of $3x^2 - 11x - 4$?
  • A$x + 4$
  • B$3x - 1$
  • C$3x + 4$
  • D$x - 4$
Show solution
Answer: D. $a \cdot c = -12$, and $-12$ and $1$ add to $-11$. So $3x^2 - 12x + x - 4 = 3x(x - 4) + 1(x - 4) = (3x + 1)(x - 4)$. Check with $x = 4$: $48 - 44 - 4 = 0$, so $x - 4$ is a factor.
Hard
Which expression is equivalent to $9x^2 - 12x + 4$?
  • A$(3x - 2)^2$
  • B$(3x + 2)^2$
  • C$(9x - 2)(x - 2)$
  • D$(3x - 4)(3x - 1)$
Show solution
Answer: A. The first and last terms are perfect squares, $(3x)^2$ and $2^2$, and the middle term is $-2(3x)(2) = -12x$. So the expression is $(3x - 2)^2$. Choice B gives $+12x$.

If $(ax - 1)(3x + b) = 6x^2 + 7x - 5$ for all values of $x$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants, what is the value of $a + b$?

Answer: $7$. Expand: $3ax^2 + (ab - 3)x - b$. Match the $x^2$ terms: $3a = 6$, so $a = 2$. Match the constants: $-b = -5$, so $b = 5$. Check the middle: $ab - 3 = 10 - 3 = 7$. So $a + b = 7$.
