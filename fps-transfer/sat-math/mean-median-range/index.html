Mean, Median and Range on the SAT

SAT Math · Problem-Solving & Data Analysis · Updated October 2026

Where I would start

The mean is the total divided by the count. The median is the middle value once the data is in order, or the average of the two middle values when the count is even. The range is the largest value minus the smallest.

The most useful single idea: mean $\times$ count $=$ total. Most of the harder mean questions are really questions about totals.

The SAT asks for these numbers directly, but it also asks how they change: what happens to the mean and the median when an outlier is removed, when a value is added, or when every value goes up by the same amount.

Reading the concept isn't enough. The score comes from practicing in the real digital SAT format and finding the exact mistakes you keep making.

What the SAT tests here

You seeDo this
A missing value and a known meanTotal $=$ mean $\times$ count. Subtract the values you know.
Two groups with different meansAdd the two totals, then divide by the combined count. Don't average the two means.
An outlierIt pulls the mean toward it. The median barely moves.
A frequency tableCount down to the middle position for the median. For the mean, multiply each value by its frequency and add.
Every value increased by $5$Mean and median go up by $5$. Range and standard deviation stay the same.
Every value doubledMean, median and range all double.

How to solve one, step by step

Example 1: a missing value

Five numbers have a mean of $20$. Four of them are $18$, $22$, $15$ and $25$. Find the fifth.

  1. Total $= 20 \times 5 = 100$.
  2. The four known values add to $80$.
  3. The fifth number is $100 - 80 = 20$.

Example 2: combining two groups

A class of $20$ has a mean score of $75$. A class of $30$ has a mean score of $80$. What is the mean of all $50$ students?

  1. Totals: $20 \times 75 = 1{,}500$ and $30 \times 80 = 2{,}400$.
  2. Combined: $\dfrac{3{,}900}{50} = 78$.
  3. Averaging the two means gives $77.5$, which ignores that the second class is bigger.

Example 3: the median from a frequency table

In a quiz, $2$ students scored $1$, $5$ scored $2$, $8$ scored $3$ and $5$ scored $4$. Find the median.

  1. There are $20$ scores, so the median is the average of the $10$th and $11$th in order.
  2. Positions $1$ to $2$ are $1$'s, $3$ to $7$ are $2$'s, $8$ to $15$ are $3$'s.
  3. The $10$th and $11$th are both $3$, so the median is $3$.

The mistakes that cost points

  • Finding the median without sorting. Put the values in order first.
  • Averaging two means. When the groups are different sizes, combine the totals instead.
  • Using frequencies as values. In a frequency table, the frequency says how many times each value appears. It is not a data value.
  • Forgetting the even-count rule. With $6$ values, the median is the average of the $3$rd and $4$th.
  • Thinking an outlier moves the median a lot. It changes the mean much more than the median.

Hard
A data set of $10$ values has a mean of $12$. When one value, $30$, is removed, what is the mean of the remaining values?
  • A$10$
  • B$9$
  • C$11$
  • D$12$

Answer: A. The total is $120$. Without $30$ it is $90$, spread over $9$ values: $\dfrac{90}{9} = 10$.
Hard
The data set $6, 7, 7, 8, 9, 40$ is changed by replacing $40$ with $10$. Which statement describes the effect?
  • AThe mean and the median both decrease.
  • BThe median decreases and the mean stays the same.
  • CThe mean decreases and the median stays the same.
  • DNeither the mean nor the median changes.
Show solution
Answer: C. The median is the average of the middle two values, $7$ and $8$, both before and after: $7.5$. The mean drops from $\dfrac{77}{6}$ to $\dfrac{47}{6}$.

Hard
Every value in a data set is increased by $5$. Which statement is true about the new data set?
  • AThe mean and the range both increase by $5$.
  • BThe mean and the median increase by $5$, and the range does not change.
  • CThe mean increases by $5$, and the median does not change.
  • DNothing changes, because every value moved by the same amount.
Show solution
Answer: B. Adding $5$ to every value moves the whole data set up by $5$, so the mean and median each increase by $5$. The gap between the largest and smallest values stays the same, so the range does not change.
