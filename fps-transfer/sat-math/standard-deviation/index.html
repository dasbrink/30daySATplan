Interpreting Standard Deviation

SAT Math · Problem-Solving & Data Analysis · Updated October 2026

The one idea to hold onto

Standard deviation measures how far the values in a data set sit from the mean. A larger standard deviation means the values are more scattered. It says nothing about whether the average is high or low.

On the SAT you almost never calculate it. You compare the spread of two data sets, or you decide what happens to the standard deviation when the data changes.

Three kinds of standard deviation questions show up on the SAT: compare two data sets, predict what a change does to the spread, and read spread from a dot plot or list. All three come down to one question: how far are the values from the middle?

Reading the concept isn't enough. The score comes from practicing in the real digital SAT format and finding the exact mistakes you keep making.

What the SAT tests here

Tightly clustered data has a small standard deviation. Widely scattered data has a large one. Two data sets can have exactly the same mean and very different standard deviations. If every value is identical, the standard deviation is $0$.

Most SAT questions about standard deviation are really questions about change. Learn these rules and you can answer them without any arithmetic.

How to solve one, step by step

Example 1: two sets with the same mean

Set A is $48, 49, 50, 51, 52$. Set B is $30, 40, 50, 60, 70$. Which has the larger standard deviation?

  1. Find the middle of each set. Both have a mean of $50$.
  2. Look at the distances from $50$. Set A: $2, 1, 0, 1, 2$. Set B: $20, 10, 0, 10, 20$.
  3. Set B's values sit ten times farther from the mean, so Set B has the larger standard deviation. No formula needed.

Example 2: removing an outlier

A data set is $12, 14, 15, 16, 43$. What happens to the mean and the standard deviation if $43$ is removed?

  1. The mean is $\frac{12 + 14 + 15 + 16 + 43}{5} = \frac{100}{5} = 20$.
  2. Without $43$, the mean is $\frac{57}{4} = 14.25$. The mean decreases.
  3. $43$ was the value farthest from the mean. The four values left are close together, so the standard deviation decreases too.

Example 3: adding the same amount to every value

Quiz scores have a mean of $62$ and a standard deviation of $4$. The teacher adds $7$ points to every score.

  1. Every score moves up by $7$, so the mean becomes $62 + 7 = 69$.
  2. Every score moves the same amount, so each score's distance from the mean is unchanged.
  3. The standard deviation stays $4$. Shifting the whole data set does not spread it out.

The empirical rule

When data is roughly bell-shaped (symmetric, with most values near the middle), the standard deviation tells you where the values fall:

  • About $68\%$ of values lie within $1$ standard deviation of the mean.
  • About $95\%$ lie within $2$ standard deviations.
  • About $99.7\%$ lie within $3$ standard deviations.

The darker band holds about $68\%$ of the data. Add the two lighter bands and the whole shaded area, within $2$ standard deviations, holds about $68\% + 13.5\% + 13.5\% = 95\%$.

Example 4: using the empirical rule

Heights of a large group of students are roughly bell-shaped with a mean of $64$ inches and a standard deviation of $3$ inches.

  1. One standard deviation on each side: $64 - 3 = 61$ and $64 + 3 = 67$. About $68\%$ of heights are between $61$ and $67$ inches.
  2. Two standard deviations on each side: $64 - 6 = 58$ and $64 + 6 = 70$. About $95\%$ are between $58$ and $70$ inches.
  3. The other $5\%$ is split evenly between the two ends, so about $2.5\%$ of students are taller than $70$ inches.

The SAT itself rarely asks about the empirical rule by name. It is still worth knowing: it turns "one standard deviation" into a picture you can use, and it is standard material in AP Statistics and on many state tests. It only works for data that is roughly bell-shaped, not for skewed data.

The mistakes that cost points

  • Linking standard deviation to the mean. A higher standard deviation means more spread, not a higher average.
  • Confusing standard deviation with range. Range uses only the two extreme values. Standard deviation reflects every value.
  • Thinking a shift changes the spread. Adding or subtracting the same number from every value moves the mean but leaves the standard deviation unchanged.
  • Ignoring outliers. One value far from the rest raises the standard deviation a lot. Removing it lowers the standard deviation.
  • Using the empirical rule on skewed data. The $68\%$, $95\%$, $99.7\%$ pattern only holds when the data is roughly bell-shaped.

Practice questions

Try these the way you would on test day, then open the solution to check your method.

Easy
Dataset X has values $10, 10, 10, 10, 10$. Dataset Y has values $2, 6, 10, 14, 18$. Which dataset has the larger standard deviation?
  • ADataset X
  • BDataset Y
  • CThey have the same standard deviation
  • DIt cannot be determined without calculating
Show solution
Answer: B. Both datasets have a mean of $10$. Every value in Dataset X equals the mean, so its standard deviation is $0$. Dataset Y's values sit as far as $8$ away from the mean, so it has the larger standard deviation.
Easy
A teacher reports that every student in a class scored exactly $78$ on a quiz. What is the standard deviation of the scores?
  • A$1$
  • B$78$
  • C$0$
  • DIt cannot be determined from the information given
Show solution
Answer: C. The mean is $78$ and every score equals the mean. No score sits any distance from the mean, so there is no spread and the standard deviation is $0$.
Medium
Two groups of students recorded their daily exercise time. Group 1 has a mean of $45$ minutes and a standard deviation of $20$ minutes. Group 2 has a mean of $30$ minutes and a standard deviation of $5$ minutes. Which group's data is more spread out?
  • AGroup 2
  • BThey are equally spread out
  • CIt cannot be determined without the data values
  • DGroup 1
Show solution
Answer: D. Standard deviation measures spread. Group 1's standard deviation of $20$ is four times Group 2's standard deviation of $5$. The means do not matter for this comparison.
Medium
A set of test scores has a mean of $62$ and a standard deviation of $4$. The teacher adds $7$ points to every score. What are the mean and standard deviation of the new scores?
  • AMean $62$, standard deviation $11$
  • BMean $69$, standard deviation $4$
  • CMean $69$, standard deviation $11$
  • DMean $62$, standard deviation $4$
Show solution
Answer: B. Every score goes up by $7$, so the mean goes up by $7$ to $69$. Every score moves the same amount, so the distances from the mean do not change and the standard deviation stays $4$.
Medium
A data set consists of the values $12, 14, 15, 16, 43$. If the value $43$ is removed, what happens to the mean and the standard deviation?
  • AThe mean decreases and the standard deviation increases.
  • BThe mean stays the same and the standard deviation decreases.
  • CBoth increase.
  • DBoth decrease.
Show solution
Answer: D. The mean drops from $\frac{100}{5} = 20$ to $\frac{57}{4} = 14.25$. The value $43$ was far from all the others, so removing it leaves values that are close together, and the standard deviation decreases.
Hard
A data set has a standard deviation of $3$. Each value in the data set is multiplied by $2$. What is the standard deviation of the new data set?
  • A$3$
  • B$5$
  • C$6$
  • D$9$
Show solution
Answer: C. Multiplying every value by $2$ also multiplies the mean by $2$, so every distance from the mean doubles. The standard deviation doubles from $3$ to $6$. Choice B comes from adding $2$ instead of multiplying.
Hard
List A is $1, 5, 9, 13, 17$. List B is $6, 7, 9, 11, 12$. Which statement is true?
  • AThe means are equal, and List B has the larger standard deviation.
  • BList A has the larger mean and the larger standard deviation.
  • CThe means are equal, and List A has the larger standard deviation.
  • DThe lists have the same mean and the same standard deviation.
Show solution
Answer: C. Both lists add to $45$, so both means are $\frac{45}{5} = 9$. List A's values sit $8, 4, 0, 4, 8$ from the mean. List B's sit $3, 2, 0, 2, 3$ from the mean. List A is more spread out, so it has the larger standard deviation.

Empirical rule practice

For each question, the data is roughly bell-shaped, so the $68\%$, $95\%$, $99.7\%$ pattern applies.

Medium
Scores on a practice test are roughly bell-shaped with a mean of $500$ and a standard deviation of $100$. About what percent of scores fall between $400$ and $600$?
  • A$34\%$
  • B$68\%$
  • C$95\%$
  • D$99.7\%$
Show solution
Answer: B. $400 = 500 - 100$ and $600 = 500 + 100$, so the interval is $1$ standard deviation on each side of the mean. About $68\%$ of scores fall there. Choice A is only the half from $500$ to $600$.
Medium
Heights of a large group of students are roughly bell-shaped with a mean of $64$ inches and a standard deviation of $3$ inches. About $95\%$ of the heights fall between which two values?
  • A$61$ and $67$ inches
  • B$58$ and $70$ inches
  • C$55$ and $73$ inches
  • D$62$ and $66$ inches
Show solution
Answer: B. About $95\%$ of values lie within $2$ standard deviations of the mean. $64 - 2(3) = 58$ and $64 + 2(3) = 70$. Choice A is $1$ standard deviation ($68\%$) and choice C is $3$ standard deviations ($99.7\%$).
Hard
The battery life of a phone model is roughly bell-shaped with a mean of $40$ hours and a standard deviation of $5$ hours. About what percent of these batteries last longer than $50$ hours?
  • A$2.5\%$
  • B$5\%$
  • C$16\%$
  • D$32\%$
Show solution
Answer: A. $50 = 40 + 2(5)$, so $50$ hours is $2$ standard deviations above the mean. About $95\%$ of batteries fall within $2$ standard deviations, which leaves $5\%$ outside. That $5\%$ splits evenly between the two ends, so about $2.5\%$ last longer than $50$ hours.
Hard
Scores on a test are roughly bell-shaped with a mean of $70$ and a standard deviation of $8$. About $16\%$ of students scored above what value?
  • A$62$
  • B$70$
  • C$78$
  • D$86$
Show solution
Answer: C. About $68\%$ of scores lie within $1$ standard deviation of the mean, so $100\% - 68\% = 32\%$ lie outside it. Half of that, $16\%$, is above the mean. One standard deviation above the mean is $70 + 8 = 78$.
