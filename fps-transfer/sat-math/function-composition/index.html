Function Composition on the SAT

SAT Math · Advanced Math · Updated October 2026

The one idea to hold onto

$f(g(x))$ means you do $g$ first and then put the result into $f$. You always work from the inside out. With a number, find $g(4)$, then find $f$ of that answer.

With an expression, replace every $x$ in $f$ with the whole of $g(x)$, in parentheses. The order matters: $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$ are usually different, and the SAT likes to put both among the answer choices.

Composition questions are short. Almost every wrong answer I see comes from doing the outer function first, or from substituting into only part of the rule.

Examples

Both orders

$f(x) = 2x + 1$ and $g(x) = x^2$. Find $f(g(x))$ and $g(f(x))$.

  1. $f(g(x)) = 2(x^2) + 1 = 2x^2 + 1$.
  2. $g(f(x)) = (2x + 1)^2 = 4x^2 + 4x + 1$.
  3. The two are different, which is why the SAT likes to offer both.

Finding the outer function

$g(x) = 2x + 1$ and $f(g(x)) = 6x + 5$, where $f$ is linear. Find $f(4)$.

  1. Write $6x + 5$ in terms of $2x + 1$: $6x + 5 = 3(2x + 1) + 2$.
  2. So $f(u) = 3u + 2$.
  3. $f(4) = 3(4) + 2 = 14$.

Solving an equation with a composition

$f(x) = x^2 - 4$ and $g(x) = x + 2$. For what values of $x$ is $f(g(x)) = 0$?

  1. $f(g(x)) = (x + 2)^2 - 4$.
  2. Set it to $0$: $(x + 2)^2 = 4$, so $x + 2 = 2$ or $x + 2 = -2$.
  3. $x = 0$ or $x = -4$.

What goes wrong

  • Working outside in. In $f(g(4))$, $g$ goes first.
  • Treating composition as multiplication. $f(g(x))$ is not $f(x) \cdot g(x)$.
  • Leaving out parentheses. $g(f(x))$ with $g(x) = x^2$ is $(2x + 1)^2$, not $2x^2 + 1$.
  • Picking the other order. The answer choices often include $g(f(x))$ when the question asks for $f(g(x))$.
  • Squaring a binomial term by term. $(2x + 1)^2 = 4x^2 + 4x + 1$, not $4x^2 + 1$.

Quick reference

You seeDo this
$f(g(4))$Find $g(4)$, then put that number into $f$.
$f(g(x))$ as an expressionReplace each $x$ in $f$ with $(g(x))$, then simplify.
$f(f(2))$Apply $f$ twice: find $f(2)$, then $f$ of that.
$f(g(x))$ given, $g$ given, find $f$Let $u = g(x)$ and rewrite the result in terms of $u$.
Domain of $f(g(x))$The inside output must be a value $f$ accepts, such as $\ge 0$ under a square root.
Tables or graphs of $f$ and $g$Read $g$ at the input first, then look up that output in $f$.

Work from the inside out on every one. Then open the solution to compare.

Easy
If $f(x) = x + 3$ and $g(x) = 2x$, what is the value of $f(g(4))$?
  • A$11$
  • B$14$
  • C$8$
  • D$24$
Show solution
Answer: A. $g(4) = 8$, then $f(8) = 11$. Choice B is $g(f(4)) = g(7) = 14$, the other order.

Hard
Let $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ and $g(x) = x - 7$. For which values of $x$ is $f(g(x))$ defined as a real number?
  • A$x \ge 0$
  • B$x \ge -7$
  • CAll real numbers
  • D$x \ge 7$
Show solution
Answer: D. $f(g(x)) = \sqrt{x - 7}$, which needs $x - 7 \ge 0$, so $x \ge 7$. Choice A is the domain of $f$ alone.
Hard
Let $f(x) = x^2 - 4$ and $g(x) = x + 2$. What are all values of $x$ for which $f(g(x)) = 0$?
  • A$0$ and $-4$
  • B$2$ and $-2$
  • C$-2$ only
  • D$0$ and $4$
Show solution
Answer: A. $(x + 2)^2 - 4 = 0$ gives $x + 2 = \pm 2$, so $x = 0$ or $x = -4$. Choice B solves $f(x) = 0$ instead of $f(g(x)) = 0$.
