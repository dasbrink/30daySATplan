The Midpoint Formula on the SAT

SAT Math · Geometry & Trigonometry · Updated October 2026

Where I would start

The midpoint is the average of the endpoints: average the $x$-coordinates, then average the $y$-coordinates. For $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ it is $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$.

When the question gives you the midpoint and one endpoint, think of the midpoint as halfway: the step from the first endpoint to the midpoint repeats once more to reach the other endpoint.

Midpoint questions are often quick points, and they also hide inside circle questions, where the center is the midpoint of a diameter. The common slip is subtracting the coordinates instead of averaging them.

How to solve one, step by step

Example 1: a missing endpoint

The midpoint of a segment is $(4, 1)$ and one endpoint is $(1, -3)$. Find the other endpoint.

  1. From $x = 1$ to the midpoint $x = 4$ is a step of $3$. Another step of $3$ reaches $x = 7$.
  2. From $y = -3$ to $y = 1$ is a step of $4$. Another step of $4$ reaches $y = 5$.
  3. The other endpoint is $(7, 5)$. Check: $\left(\dfrac{1 + 7}{2}, \dfrac{-3 + 5}{2}\right) = (4, 1)$.

Example 2: the center and radius of a circle

A diameter of a circle has endpoints $(-2, 6)$ and $(4, -2)$. Find the center and the radius.

  1. Center: $\left(\dfrac{-2 + 4}{2}, \dfrac{6 + (-2)}{2}\right) = (1, 2)$.
  2. Diameter length: $\sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10$.
  3. Radius: $5$.

Example 3: unknown coordinates

The midpoint of $(a, 3)$ and $(9, b)$ is $(5, -1)$. Find $a + b$.

  1. $x$: $\dfrac{a + 9}{2} = 5$, so $a = 1$.
  2. $y$: $\dfrac{3 + b}{2} = -1$, so $b = -5$.
  3. $a + b = -4$.

The mistakes that cost points

  • Subtracting instead of adding. The midpoint averages the coordinates. The difference belongs to the distance formula.
  • Forgetting to divide by $2$. $(2 + 8, 4 + 10) = (10, 14)$ is not the midpoint.
  • Mixing up $x$ and $y$. Average the first coordinates together and the second coordinates together.
  • Treating the midpoint as an endpoint. When one endpoint is missing, step past the midpoint, not back to it.
  • Giving the diameter as the radius. The radius is half the distance between the endpoints of a diameter.

Point $P$ lies on the segment from $(0, 0)$ to $(12, 8)$, one quarter of the way from $(0, 0)$. What are the coordinates of $P$?
  • A$(6, 4)$
  • B$(4, 3)$
  • C$(9, 6)$
  • D$(3, 2)$
Show solution
Answer: D. A quarter of the run is $\dfrac{12}{4} = 3$ and a quarter of the rise is $\dfrac{8}{4} = 2$, so $P = (3, 2)$. Choice A is the midpoint, and choice C is three quarters of the way.
