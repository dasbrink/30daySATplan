Quadratic Models on the SAT

SAT Math · Advanced Math · Updated October 2026

Where I would start

A quadratic model rises and then falls, or falls and then rises. On the SAT it is usually the height of a thrown object, a profit, or an area. Three points answer almost every question: the starting value, the vertex, and the zeros.

For $h(t) = at^2 + bt + c$: the starting value is $h(0) = c$, the vertex is at $t = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$, and the zeros are where $h(t) = 0$. Decide which of the three the question is asking for before you calculate anything.

Quadratic models are a favorite for the hard questions in the second module, because each one hides several different questions in one formula. The math is the same every time. The skill is matching the words in the question to the right feature of the graph.

Reading the concept isn't enough. The score comes from practicing in the real digital SAT format and finding the exact mistakes you keep making.

$h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t + 5$. The starting height is $h(0) = 5$, the highest point is the vertex $(2, 69)$, and the ball lands at the positive zero, about $t = 4.1$.

What the SAT tests here

The question asksFind this
"Initial height", "launched from", "fixed cost"The value at $0$: the constant $c$.
"Maximum height", "greatest profit"The output at the vertex. Find $t = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$, then put it back into the model.
"When does it reach its highest point?"The input at the vertex, $t = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$.
"When does it hit the ground?"The positive zero: solve $h(t) = 0$.
Vertex form $a(t - h)^2 + k$The vertex is $(h, k)$. You can read it without any calculation.
"For how long is it above $16.7$ meters?"Solve $h(t) = 16.7$, then subtract the two times.

How to solve one, step by step

Example 1: the three key values

A ball's height in feet after $t$ seconds is $h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t + 5$. Find the starting height and the maximum height.

  1. Starting height: $h(0) = 5$ feet.
  2. Vertex: $t = -\dfrac{64}{2(-16)} = 2$ seconds.
  3. Maximum height: $h(2) = -16(4) + 128 + 5 = 69$ feet. The answer $2$ is when, and $69$ is how high. Read which one the question wants.

Example 2: when it hits the ground

A rock's height in meters is $h(t) = -5t^2 + 20t + 25$. When does it hit the ground?

  1. Set $h(t) = 0$ and divide by $-5$: $t^2 - 4t - 5 = 0$.
  2. Factor: $(t - 5)(t + 1) = 0$, so $t = 5$ or $t = -1$.
  3. Time cannot be negative, so the rock lands after $5$ seconds.

Example 3: a maximum in a money problem

A store's revenue at price $p$ dollars is $R(p) = p(200 - 4p)$. What price gives the most revenue?

  1. The zeros are $p = 0$ and $p = 50$.
  2. A parabola is symmetric, so the vertex is halfway between the zeros: $p = 25$.
  3. The maximum revenue is $R(25) = 25 \cdot 100 = 2{,}500$ dollars.

Example 4: building the model from three values

A quadratic $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ has $f(0) = 10$, $f(1) = 13$ and $f(2) = 12$. Find $f(3)$.

  1. $f(0) = c = 10$.
  2. $f(1)$: $a + b + 10 = 13$, so $a + b = 3$. $f(2)$: $4a + 2b + 10 = 12$, so $2a + b = 1$.
  3. Subtract: $a = -2$, then $b = 5$. So $f(3) = -18 + 15 + 10 = 7$.

The mistakes that cost points

  • Giving the time when the question asks for the height. The vertex has two numbers. "When" is the first, "how high" is the second.
  • Keeping a negative zero. In a time or length problem, throw away the negative solution.
  • Sign slips in $-\dfrac{b}{2a}$. With $a = -16$ and $b = 64$, the vertex is at $t = 2$, not $-2$.
  • Reading vertex form backward. In $-16(t - 3)^2 + 150$, the vertex is $(3, 150)$, not $(-3, 150)$, and the starting height is $h(0)$, not $150$.
  • Calling $c$ the maximum. The constant is only the value at $0$. The maximum is at the vertex.
