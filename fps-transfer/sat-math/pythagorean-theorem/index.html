The Pythagorean Theorem on the SAT

SAT Math · Geometry & Trigonometry · Updated October 2026

Where I would start

In a right triangle with legs $a$ and $b$ and hypotenuse $c$, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. The hypotenuse is the side across from the right angle, and it is always the longest side.

Before you calculate, look for a triple you already know: $3$-$4$-$5$, $5$-$12$-$13$, $8$-$15$-$17$, $7$-$24$-$25$, and their multiples, like $6$-$8$-$10$ or $9$-$12$-$15$. Spotting one saves a full calculation.

The Pythagorean theorem is on the SAT reference sheet, but the questions rarely say "use the Pythagorean theorem". It hides inside ladders, diagonals of rectangles, distances between points, and the heights of isosceles triangles.

How to solve one, step by step

Example 1: a ladder

A $25$-foot ladder leans against a wall with its base $7$ feet from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?

  1. The ladder is the hypotenuse: $7^2 + h^2 = 25^2$.
  2. $h^2 = 625 - 49 = 576$.
  3. $h = 24$ feet. This is the $7$-$24$-$25$ triple.

Example 2: the height of an isosceles triangle

An isosceles triangle has sides $13$, $13$ and $10$. Find its area.

  1. The height cuts the base of $10$ into two halves of $5$.
  2. Each half is a right triangle with hypotenuse $13$ and leg $5$: $h = \sqrt{169 - 25} = 12$.
  3. Area $= \dfrac{1}{2}(10)(12) = 60$.

Example 3: distance between points

Find the distance between $(1, 2)$ and $(7, 10)$.

  1. The run is $7 - 1 = 6$ and the rise is $10 - 2 = 8$.
  2. $d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$.
  3. The $6$-$8$-$10$ triple again.

The mistakes that cost points

  • Adding when you should subtract. If you know the hypotenuse, the missing leg is $\sqrt{c^2 - a^2}$.
  • Putting the wrong side as $c$. The hypotenuse is across from the right angle, never one of the legs.
  • Forgetting the square root. $6^2 + 8^2 = 100$ is $c^2$. The side is $10$.
  • Adding the sides before squaring. $(a + b)^2$ is not $a^2 + b^2$.
  • Using the full base in an isosceles triangle. The height splits the base in half.
