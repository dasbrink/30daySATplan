The problem

Why more practice tests often don't help

"If you want to fatten a pig, it does not help to weigh it all the time."

Old farming proverb — and exactly what most SAT prep gets wrong

Taking test after test without doing anything differently in between is not preparation. It is repetition of the same mistakes.

There are many websites that will give your child more and more practice problems. But volume alone is not the solution. The SAT is a diagnostic instrument — and so should your preparation be.

This program is built specifically for diagnosing and improving your child's weaknesses and blind spots. Every single one of the 4,736 questions in the database is linked to the underlying basic math skills required to answer it, as well as to the specific SAT topic it tests. When your child takes a practice test, the program gets to work — identifying the exact areas where their knowledge is breaking down.

Then, instead of just moving on to the next test, it does something about it.

What happens when a gap is found

Four steps to actually fix a weak spot

After each diagnostic test, the program identifies your child's areas for growth and walks them through a structured remediation process for each one.

A focused tutorial on the topic

Your child gets a clear explanation of the concept they are struggling with — not a generic review, but targeted instruction on the specific skill that is costing them points.

The typical errors students make

Most wrong answers on the SAT come from a small set of predictable mistakes. Your child is shown exactly what those are for their weak topic, so they recognize the trap when they see it.

Three worked practice problems — Easy, Medium, Hard

A step-by-step walkthrough of three graded problems on the same topic. Not just the answer — the method, explained clearly.

More practice problems on the same concept

If your child wants to keep drilling the skill, more problems are available on demand — all linked to the same underlying concept.

The program structure

30 days. Four domains. Three diagnostics.

The program is structured to build skills systematically across the four SAT Math domains, with full diagnostic tests at key checkpoints to measure progress and redirect the remaining days toward what matters most.

Day 1Baseline diagnostic

A full practice SAT Math test to establish your child's starting point and identify the first round of weak spots.

Days 2 – 11Domain practice

10 days of targeted domain work, weighted toward the topics that carry the most points: Algebra, Advanced Math, Data Analysis, Geometry.

Day 12Second diagnostic

A second full test to measure progress. Results redirect the next phase toward whichever weaknesses remain.

Days 13 – 20Weakness focus

3 days targeting specific gaps from the second diagnostic, followed by a second domain practice round across all four areas.

Day 21Third diagnostic

A third full test. The remaining nine days are entirely shaped by what this test reveals.

Days 22 – 30Final push

Six days of targeted weakness work, followed by three full tests to build test-day confidence and timing.
