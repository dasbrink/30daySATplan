The Best Free SAT Math Practice Sites in 2026, Compared

By Dr. Daniel Brink, Ph.D. in Mathematics Education, University of Georgia · October 12, 2026

Where I would start: with the free official tools. Take a full-length practice test in Bluebook to see where you stand, then practice your weak skills with the College Board Student Question Bank and Khan Academy. If you want a live tutor at no cost, join a Schoolhouse.world bootcamp. If you need more full-length math tests, or want to know the exact mistake behind each question you miss, add FreePracticeSAT. Paid sites such as UWorld and OnePrep are worth it mainly for students who have used up the free material.

Full disclosure: I built FreePracticeSAT, one of the eight sites below. I have tried to be fair to every site, including the ones that compete with mine, and to be plain about where mine falls short. Prices and free features were checked on each site on October 12, 2026, and they change, so check before you pay for anything.

1. Bluebook (College Board)

Free

Bluebook is the College Board app you will take the real SAT in, and it holds the official full-length practice tests. They are timed like the real test and adaptive, so the second math module gets easier or harder depending on how you did in the first. After a test you can see your scores and review the questions in My Practice, and send yourself to Khan Academy for targeted work.

Good

  • The real questions, the real timing, the real app
  • The closest thing to a true score estimate
  • Built-in Desmos, exactly as on test day

Not so good

  • Only a limited number of full tests, so use them carefully
  • A retake repeats questions you have already seen

Best for: your first baseline test and your last few rehearsals before test day.

2. College Board Student Question Bank

Free

The Student Question Bank holds thousands of official questions. You can filter them by section, content domain, skill and difficulty, so you can spend an hour on exactly one type of question. You sign in with a College Board account.

Good

  • Official questions, sorted by skill and difficulty
  • Answer explanations included

Not so good

  • No full-length tests
  • You have to know which skill to pick, and the skill names are not always obvious

Best for: targeted practice once you know which skill is weak.

3. Khan Academy Official SAT Prep

Free

Khan Academy's SAT course was built in partnership with College Board. It covers every skill at three levels (Foundations, Medium and Advanced), with short video walk-throughs, quizzes, and a Course Challenge that helps you decide where to start. It can also walk you through every question on a Bluebook practice test you have taken.

Good

  • Teaches the math, not just tests it
  • Short videos for every skill
  • Completely free

Not so good

  • Self-directed, so it is easy to spend time on skills you already know
  • Full-length tests are taken in Bluebook, not on Khan Academy

Best for: learning or relearning a skill from the beginning.

For a closer look at how Khan Academy and FreePracticeSAT differ, see freepracticeSAT.com vs Khan Academy.

4. Schoolhouse.world SAT Bootcamps

Free

Schoolhouse.world is a nonprofit founded by Sal Khan. In partnership with College Board, it runs free SAT bootcamps on Zoom: small groups that meet twice a week for four weeks before each SAT date, led by trained peer tutors who scored 700 or higher on that section.

Good

  • A live person to ask, at no cost
  • A fixed schedule keeps you working

Not so good

  • Tutors are high school and college students, not teachers
  • Bootcamps follow the SAT calendar, so you sign up by a deadline

Best for: students who work better with a person and a group.

5. FreePracticeSAT (my site)

Free tests

FreePracticeSAT gives you unlimited full-length SAT math tests in the real format: two timed modules of 22 questions, a built-in Desmos calculator, and a choice of easy, medium, hard or mixed difficulty. It draws on more than 4,700 practice questions. What it does differently is the diagnosis. For each question you miss, it names the specific mistake behind your wrong answer, and after a second test it shows the basic skills underneath those mistakes. A free Foundations course checks the basics the SAT assumes you know. Worked solutions for every question and the full diagnosis cost \$29, one time.

Good

  • Unlimited free full-length math tests
  • Names the exact mistake behind each wrong answer
  • Pick your difficulty before every test

Not so good

  • Math only, no Reading and Writing
  • Not official College Board questions, and scores are estimates
  • No phone app

Best for: students who are stuck at the same score and want to know exactly why.

6. OnePrep

Limited free plan

OnePrep has a large bank of more than 4,000 digital SAT questions, full-length tests, a study planner, analytics, and an AI tutor that explains questions. The free plan is limited: some practice questions and one full-length test. Paid plans are one-time payments that run until a test date. For the November 2026 SAT, Plus costs \$115 and Pro costs \$131.66.

Good

  • A big question bank covering both sections
  • Detailed analytics and a study planner

Not so good

  • Most of it is behind the paid plans
  • Explanations lean on an AI tutor

Best for: students who want one paid platform for both sections.

7. UWorld

Paid, with a 7-day trial

UWorld is not free, but it is the paid site students mention most, so it belongs on the list as a benchmark. Its question bank has more than 1,700 SAT-style questions, and each one comes with a step-by-step explanation that also covers why the wrong answers are wrong. The question bank starts at \$99 and the full course at \$299. There is a 7-day free trial.

Good

  • Very thorough explanations
  • An interface modeled on Bluebook

Not so good

  • No free plan beyond the trial
  • Not official questions

Best for: strong students who have used up the official material.

8. PrepScholar blog

Free articles

PrepScholar sells a paid SAT program, but its blog has free written guides on every SAT math topic, plus lists of other free resources.

Good

  • Clear written guides on each topic
  • Free to read, no account

Not so good

  • Reading, not practice
  • The articles point you toward the paid program

Best for: reading a quick explanation of one topic.

How I would use them together

You do not need all eight. As a math teacher, this is the order I would give a student who has a month or more:

  1. Take one Bluebook practice test to get an honest starting score.
  2. Find out why you lost points. A score tells you how many you missed, not why. Look at which skills the missed questions test, or take a diagnostic that names the mistakes for you.
  3. Fix the weakest skills first with Khan Academy lessons and Question Bank questions on those skills only.
  4. Take more full-length math tests to build speed and stamina, and keep a few Bluebook tests for the final weeks.
  5. Finish with Bluebook under real timing, a week or two before test day.

Paying for a site makes the most sense once you have worked through the free material and still need more questions. Many students reach a strong score without paying for anything.
