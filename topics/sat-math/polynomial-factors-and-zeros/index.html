Polynomial Factors and Zeros

SAT Math · Advanced Math · Updated October 2026

These questions use a handful of words, zero, root, factor, $x$-intercept, solution, that all point at the same idea. Students who see that they are one idea answer quickly. Students who treat them as four different topics spend a long time, and that is time the second module does not give you.

The one idea to hold onto

If $p(a) = 0$, then $a$ is a zero of $p$, $x = a$ is an $x$-intercept of its graph, and $(x - a)$ is a factor of $p(x)$. Each of these says the same thing.

When you divide $p(x)$ by $(x - a)$, the remainder is $p(a)$. So to test a factor, or find a remainder, just substitute.

Examples, step by step

Finding a missing coefficient

$p(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + kx - 6$, and $(x - 3)$ is a factor of $p(x)$. What is $k$?

  1. A factor of $(x - 3)$ means $p(3) = 0$.
  2. $27 - 18 + 3k - 6 = 0$, so $3 + 3k = 0$.
  3. $k = -1$.

A remainder without dividing

What is the remainder when $x^3 + 2x - 5$ is divided by $x - 1$?

  1. The remainder is $p(1)$.
  2. $1 + 2 - 5 = -2$.
  3. No long division needed.

Building from zeros

A polynomial has zeros $-3$, $1$ and $5$, and $p(0) = 30$. What is $p(2)$?

  1. Write $p(x) = a(x + 3)(x - 1)(x - 5)$.
  2. $p(0) = a(3)(-1)(-5) = 15a = 30$, so $a = 2$.
  3. $p(2) = 2(5)(1)(-3) = -30$.

What goes wrong

  • The sign of the zero. The factor $(x + 2)$ gives the zero $-2$, not $2$.
  • Forgetting the leading coefficient. Zeros alone do not fix a polynomial. $2(x - 1)(x - 3)$ and $(x - 1)(x - 3)$ have the same zeros.
  • Long division when substitution would do. The remainder theorem turns a long calculation into one substitution.
  • Missing a zero at $0$. In $x^3 - 4x = x(x - 2)(x + 2)$, the factor $x$ gives the zero $0$.

Where you will see it

  • Which is a factor? Substitute the zero each choice implies.
  • Find the constant. Set $p(a) = 0$ or $p(a) = $ the remainder, and solve.
  • Read zeros from a graph. Where the graph meets the $x$-axis. A bounce means even multiplicity.
  • Count the solutions. Factor completely, then count distinct real zeros.
