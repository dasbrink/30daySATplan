Translating Word Problems into Equations

SAT Math · Algebra · Updated October 2026

Students who are good at algebra still lose points on word problems, because the algebra is the easy half. The hard half is writing the right equation from a paragraph. I teach this the same way every year: decide what the letter stands for, translate one phrase at a time, and check the answer against the story, not just the equation.

Before you write anything

Write down in words what your variable means, including its units. "Let $h$ be the number of hours worked" saves you from solving for the wrong thing.

Then translate phrase by phrase. "Less than" reverses the order: $5$ less than $2n$ is $2n - 5$. "Of" with a percent or fraction means multiply. "Per" means divide, or a rate that multiplies.

Three examples

A fixed fee and a rate

A plumber charges $\$45$ for a visit plus $\$30$ per hour. A bill comes to $\$165$. How many hours did the plumber work?

  1. Let $h$ be the number of hours.
  2. $45 + 30h = 165$.
  3. $30h = 120$, so $h = 4$ hours.
  4. Check against the story: $45 + 120 = 165$.

Two unknowns, two facts

A school sold $200$ tickets to a play. Adult tickets cost $\$12$ and student tickets cost $\$7$. The school collected $\$2{,}000$. How many student tickets were sold?

  1. Let $a$ be adult tickets and $s$ be student tickets.
  2. Count of tickets: $a + s = 200$. Money: $12a + 7s = 2{,}000$.
  3. Replace $a$ with $200 - s$: $2{,}400 - 12s + 7s = 2{,}000$, so $5s = 400$ and $s = 80$.
  4. The question asks for student tickets, so the answer is $80$, not the $120$ adult tickets.

An age problem

A mother is $4$ times as old as her son. In $20$ years she will be twice as old as he is. How old is the son now?

  1. Let $s$ be the son's age now. The mother is $4s$.
  2. In $20$ years: $4s + 20 = 2(s + 20)$.
  3. $4s + 20 = 2s + 40$, so $s = 10$.
  4. Check: $40$ and $10$ now, $60$ and $30$ later. Twice as old.

Small slips that cost points

  • Reversing "less than". "$7$ less than $x$" is $x - 7$. Students write $7 - x$ because the words come in that order.
  • Solving for the wrong quantity. If you let $a$ be adult tickets and the question asks for student tickets, finish the job.
  • Forgetting parentheses. "Twice the sum of $n$ and $3$" is $2(n + 3)$, not $2n + 3$.
  • Adding the years to only one person. In age problems, both people get older.
  • Mixing units. If a rate is per minute and the time is in hours, convert before you write the equation.

What you will see

  • Write the equation or expression. Several choices look alike. Test each against one number from the story.
  • Solve a one-variable story. A fee plus a rate, a total split into parts, consecutive integers.
  • Solve a two-variable story. Tickets, coins, mixtures. One equation counts things, the other counts money or weight.
  • Interpret a number. What does the $30$ in $45 + 30h$ mean? It is the cost per hour.

Easy
Five less than twice a number is $17$. What is the number?

  • B$11$
  • C$12$
  • D$22$
Show solution
Answer: B. $2n - 5 = 17$, so $2n = 22$ and $n = 11$. Choice D stops at $2n$.

Medium
A phone plan costs $\$20$ per month plus $\$0.10$ for each text message. Which expression gives the total cost, in dollars, for $m$ months in which a total of $t$ text messages are sent?
  • A$20m + 0.1t$
  • B$20 + 0.1t$
  • C$0.1m + 20t$
  • D$20.1mt$
Show solution
Answer: A. The monthly fee is paid $m$ times, and each of the $t$ texts costs $\$0.10$: $20m + 0.1t$. Choice B charges the monthly fee only once.
MediumStudent-produced response
The sum of three consecutive integers is $141$. What is the smallest of the three integers?
Enter your answer.
Show solution
Answer: $46$. $n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 141$, so $3n + 3 = 141$ and $n = 46$.
Medium
A school sold $200$ tickets to a play. Adult tickets cost $\$12$ and student tickets cost $\$7$. The school collected $\$2{,}000$ in all. How many student tickets were sold?
  • A$80$
  • B$100$
  • C$120$
  • D$160$
Show solution
Answer: A. $a + s = 200$ and $12a + 7s = 2{,}000$. Then $12(200 - s) + 7s = 2{,}000$, so $s = 80$. Choice C is the number of adult tickets.
Medium
The number $x$ is $30\%$ of the number $y$. Which equation shows $x$ in terms of $y$?
  • A$x = \dfrac{y}{0.3}$
  • B$x = 0.3y$
  • C$x = 30y$
  • D$x = y + 0.3$
Show solution
Answer: B. "Of" means multiply, and $30\%$ is $0.3$: $x = 0.3y$. Choice A gives $y$ in terms of $x$ by mistake.
HardStudent-produced response
The sum of a positive number and its reciprocal is $\dfrac{10}{3}$. If the number is greater than $1$, what is the number?
Enter your answer.
Show solution
Answer: $3$. $n + \dfrac{1}{n} = \dfrac{10}{3}$. Multiply by $3n$: $3n^2 - 10n + 3 = 0$, so $(3n - 1)(n - 3) = 0$. The solutions are $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $3$, and the one greater than $1$ is $3$.
Hard
A mother is $4$ times as old as her son. In $20$ years, she will be twice as old as her son. How old is the son now?
  • A$10$
  • B$5$
  • C$20$
  • D$40$
Show solution
Answer: A. $4s + 20 = 2(s + 20)$ gives $s = 10$. Choice D is the mother's age now.
Hard
The length of a rectangle is $3$ more than twice its width. The perimeter of the rectangle is $54$. What is the area of the rectangle?
  • A$112$
  • B$128$
  • C$152$
  • D$171$
Show solution
Answer: C. $2(w + 2w + 3) = 54$, so $6w + 6 = 54$ and $w = 8$. The length is $19$ and the area is $8 \cdot 19 = 152$.
HardStudent-produced response
Tank A holds $500$ liters of water and drains at $12$ liters per minute. Tank B holds $200$ liters and fills at $18$ liters per minute. After how many minutes will the two tanks hold the same amount of water?
Enter your answer.
Show solution
Answer: $10$. $500 - 12t = 200 + 18t$, so $300 = 30t$ and $t = 10$. Check: both tanks hold $380$ liters.
