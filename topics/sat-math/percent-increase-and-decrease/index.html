Percent Increase and Decrease

SAT Math · Problem-Solving and Data Analysis · Updated October 2026

Percent questions show up in every SAT, in the calculator section and out of it, inside word problems and tables. The math is simple. Points get lost because students divide by the new number instead of the original, or add and subtract percents that should be multiplied.

The two tools

Percent change is $\dfrac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \times 100$. Always divide by the original, the number you started from.

To increase by $15\%$, multiply by $1.15$. To decrease by $15\%$, multiply by $0.85$. Multipliers are the fastest way through almost every question, and they make changes in a row easy: just multiply them.

Worked examples

Finding the percent change

A town's population grew from $400$ to $500$. What was the percent increase?

  1. The change is $500 - 400 = 100$.
  2. Divide by the original: $\dfrac{100}{400} = 0.25$.
  3. That is a $25\%$ increase. Dividing by $500$ gives $20\%$, which is the classic wrong answer.

Applying a change

A jacket costs $\$80$. It is on sale for $25\%$ off. What is the sale price?

  1. A $25\%$ decrease leaves $75\%$, so the multiplier is $0.75$.
  2. $80 \cdot 0.75 = 60$.
  3. The sale price is $\$60$. The discount itself, $\$20$, is the other choice you will see.

An increase followed by a decrease

A price rises $20\%$ and then falls $20\%$. What is the overall percent change?

  1. Multiply the multipliers: $1.2 \cdot 0.8 = 0.96$.
  2. The final price is $96\%$ of the start, a $4\%$ decrease.
  3. It is not $0\%$. The second $20\%$ is taken from a bigger number than the first.

Comparing two quantities

Ana earns $25\%$ more than Ben. Ben earns what percent less than Ana?

  1. Write the relationship with a multiplier: $A = 1.25B$.
  2. So $B = \dfrac{A}{1.25} = 0.8A$.
  3. Ben earns $20\%$ less than Ana. The percent changes because the base changes: $25\%$ of Ben's pay is a smaller amount than $25\%$ of Ana's.

What you will be asked

  • Find the percent change. New minus original, over original.
  • Find the new value. Multiply the original by the multiplier.
  • Two changes in a row. Multiply the two multipliers. Never add the percents.
  • Compare two quantities. "$p$ is $25\%$ more than $q$" means $p = 1.25q$. Going the other way gives a different percent.
  • Write the expression. Questions with letters instead of numbers. Try a number in each choice if the algebra feels slippery.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by the new value. From $250$ to $200$ is a $20\%$ decrease. Dividing $50$ by $200$ gives $25\%$, which is wrong.
  • Adding percents in a row. Up $10\%$ then up $20\%$ is $1.1 \cdot 1.2 = 1.32$, a $32\%$ increase, not $30\%$.
  • Using the decrease as the multiplier. A $7\%$ decrease multiplies by $0.93$, not $0.07$.
  • Assuming percent comparisons reverse. If $p$ is $25\%$ more than $q$, then $q$ is $20\%$ less than $p$, not $25\%$ less.
